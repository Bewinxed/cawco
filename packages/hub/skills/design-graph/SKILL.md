# Design graph

The **design graph** is the project's real component or screen, mounted live by the project's own toolchain (its **stage**) and opened in the person's panel by `artifact_create` with `kind: "component"`. Each story of a **states file** (`<Component>.stories.ts`, CSF 3, beside the component) is a frame, and each frame is drawn again in every interaction state its CSS has a rule for (hover, focus, pressed), derived, never authored. Beside the frame the panel holds its States, Map, Animation and Controls faces, and its View sets the width, the colour scheme and reduced motion. You see what you drew: the stage's page reports every frame, and every tool answers from that **report**, never from the files alone. The `show` skill's step 1 comes first.

Something not in the codebase yet, or a throwaway mockup, opens on the graph as an **HTML page** the hub keeps, with no stage behind it: read `references/html.md` and skip the steps below. A piece that exists, or that you are building now, is a live story, as follows.

**The person's graph is live.** Once it is open in their panel it shows your workspace's stage as it is: every file you save reaches their open graph within a second through hot reload, with no `artifact_update`. Save each file whole and compiling. A save that breaks a story shows in its frame as an error card in place of the drawing, and your next save that works brings the story back by itself. A section that fails, or a story that throws as it draws, reaches you as the stage's error.

A piece has two kinds of states file, for two readers:

- **The live story** is the person's: one story that mounts the piece as the product composes it, for them to operate. A press does what it does in the product, answered by mock data, and a dial sets each situation no press reaches.
- **The states** are your reading: every state the code reaches, one story each, in the states file of the part that draws it. They stay yours; the person reaches each by using the live story.

One design, a piece or a whole screen, reaches the person on its design graph. Two or more directions open in one design graph, one states file each, and they pick one there with the head's Pick, which `artifact_read` answers as `picked`.

## Steps

1. **Read the map.** `design_map` with `dir` (or `query`) for the folder you build in; `component` for any part you might use. Done when you know which existing components this one is made of and which product components it ships in (its used in): you **mount** those; their markup stays theirs (`references/map.md`). On a clean-slate redesign the surface being replaced is out of the map: mount only the design system's primitives and write every part above them new.
2. **Write the component** props in, events out: what it shows arrives as props and what the person does leaves as events, so a story feeds it any state and a fixture answers what it sends. The component follows its props while mounted: a story's args are inputs the live instance takes at any time, never a record it copies into its own `$state` as it opens (the `house/follows-props` lint catches the copy). A change to a component the product already has is made in that component, in your workspace: nothing ships until your work lands, so the story mounts the real one and no twin is written beside it. Done when its build and type-check pass.
3. **Write the states files**, each `export default { title: "Group/Name", component, args }` with what every story shares; `title` is the section's heading.
   - **The live story is what they are handed**: a states file whose one story mounts the piece as the product composes it (the real picker in its real card, from the map). A press then does what it does in the product: the menu opens in its own popover, a pick shows its wait and then its answer. A situation no press reaches (what the server answers, how old a reading is, a machine away) is an arg with a dial (step 4). A piece nothing is pressed in (a badge, a meter) has its live story too: it stands in the surface that holds it, and its dials set the args that change it. A whole screen has its live story too: one story that mounts the screen once inside the app's shell (its sidebar, tabs and panels, from the map), fed the same mock.
   - **A fixture mounts and mocks**: where the piece is several product components, or sends events, the live story's `component` is a fixture beside it. The states file keeps the piece's name and the fixture one of its own (`LimitPicker.stories.ts` draws `limit-picker-live.svelte`). It holds the product's components with their props, the mock that feeds them, and wrappers that give the frame room. Everything a person sees or presses in the frame is drawn by a product component (the `house/fixture-mounts` lint refuses a control, a `role` or a kit recipe a fixture writes itself). It follows its args as any component does (step 2), and its own `$state` holds only what the mock server would hold: which row is being asked, what came back. Keyed on an `action` dial's arg (Svelte's `{#key again}`), the piece starts over at their press.
   - **The mock stands at the component's data**, never in the component's place (`references/mocks.md` has each one's mechanics):

     | The component takes it from | The mock |
     | --- | --- |
     | A prop | The story's `args`; the fixture's `$state` for what a press changes |
     | An event it sends | The fixture's handler, through the prop that takes the event: the wait, then the answer its dial names |
     | A store module | Written through the store's own writers in the story's `beforeEach` |
     | A store behind a provider | A decorator per story that makes and provides the instance |
     | The server's reads | The project's stand-in for its server, as a decorator (the dashboard's `hubAs`) |
     | A call the server lacks yet | One new function in the store the component reads; the mock stands at that function, and your report names it for the build |

   - **A layer the component portals out of the story** (a popover, a menu, a tooltip, a dialog) stays the component's own: the stage places it against the frame, and a picture shows it once the frame has room for it. The report reads the story's root alone, so what the layer holds is read in its part's own states file, where that part mounts by itself.
   - **The states are your reading**: each is a situation the person meets that the code reaches, one export in the states file of the part that draws it (a row's states in the row's file), named as the product talks (`Waiting`, `Denied`), with only the args it changes. Each draws a picture none of the others draws; two that draw alike are one state, so keep one. Keep those that differ to the eye (the result at rest, the empty, the longest, the failure they recover from) and leave out a branch nobody would tell from another. Each stop of a dial is also a story of your reading, mounting the live fixture with that arg, so every setting they can reach is one you have read.
   - **The label is what they would say**: a one-line doc comment above each story, the sentence they would say looking at that frame, `/** Waiting for the phone to scan the code. */`. It names the situation in their words: no prop, export or field names, no list of what is on screen, no width. It is the frame's label; the live story's says the situation it opens in.
   - **The frame is its container**: the stage draws the story's root against its frame's edge, so a part that lives inside a padded surface in the product (a row in a list, a chip in a bar, a card's body) is mounted in that surface's own component from the map, through the story's `decorators` or a fixture. A whole surface brings its own.
   - **Width and scheme are the graph's**: each story is one frame, and the graph's View draws it at phone, tablet, desk and wide width and in either scheme at their press, so one state is one story. Read each frame yourself at every one of those widths and in both schemes (`artifact_read` with `state.width` and `state.scheme`) before you hand it up. A piece with one production width (a phone sheet) names it once, as the meta's `parameters: { viewport: "phone" }` (or `"tablet"`, `"desk"`, a width).
   - **A value is a dial, a situation is a dial, a structure is a story**: what varies along a scale (a density, a radius, a duration, how many rows) is one `number` or `select` dial on the live story, bounded to the range that holds (`min`, `max`, `step`, or the named stops as `options`). What the server or the world decides is a `select` or `toggle` dial. Only a different arrangement of parts is a story of its own, and a direction a states file of its own. A value that depends on another is derived from it in the component's CSS (`calc()` on the dialled variable, or the token it sets), so one dial moves both and the shipped component keeps the rule.
   - **A flow between states**: `from: "Waiting"` and a `play` on the destination story that calls `update`, so the live instance moves and its own motion plays (`references/moves.md`). Motion they are asked to tune is a move on the live story: a second story in the live states file, `from` the live story, whose `play` makes the press that sets it off.
   - **The poster**: the graph opens on the frame that tells the story on its own, the result on screen and at rest, never mid-move; it is the first export. A `steps` sequence names its payoff step.
   - **The beats**: `steps`, or a chain of `from`, is three to five states and a payoff, in the order the person meets them; more only when they clarify.

   Done when the piece has its live story, where a press reaches every situation a press reaches in the product and a dial sets each of the rest; every state you read is one export with its sentence in its part's states file; each file's first export is its poster; and the build passes.
4. **Open two design graphs**, each `artifact_create({ kind: "component", name, title, does: "judge", ask, file })` (the hub refuses `try` on a component).
   - **Your reading**: `reading: true`, and `file` lists the parts' states files (`file: [..]`, each its own section, loaded as it comes into view). It stays in your own panel for you to read with `artifact_read`, and is deleted when your work item ends. Its `ask` says what it covers ("Every state of the pairing rows"). Add `edges` and `group` when the frames read better as a graph. Every graph you open for your own checking (a probe, a motion check, a pasted reading) takes `reading: true` the same way.
   - **The person's graph**: one per screen. When `artifact_list` already holds a graph of this screen in the panel yours goes to, whichever session of your delegation tree made it, `name` is its name, and your create puts your version in its place, picks and pins kept; `artifact_read` then tells the earlier writer its version moved. `file` is the live story's states file, or with directions one live states file per direction, each named with its line (`directions: [{ file: "RowQuiet.stories.ts", name: "Quiet", line: "Rows at rest, the state in a glyph" }, { file: "RowDense.stories.ts", name: "Dense", line: "Twice the rows a screen" }]`; `file` may then be left out). `title` names the piece in plain words ("Pairing a phone"); `ask` names what to do in it and what to pin ("Judge pairing a phone: scan the code, approve it, and pin what reads wrong"). `dials` hold each situation no press reaches, each setting an `arg`: `select` for which answer the server gives, `toggle` for a condition, `action` to start again (its arg counts their presses). A dial for a value they tune (step 8) sets a `cssVar` on the drawing or an `arg`; a dial's `group` is the heading it stands under.

   Done when each answers an id.
5. **Read what was drawn.** The answer's `render` lists every frame: `drawn`, `empty`, `failed` (with its `file:line:column`) or `unseen`. Fix each `failed` and `empty` frame in the component or the story and read again (`artifact_read`, `render: true` to draw it now in the machine's browser). For each forced state (`references/states.md`), read its delta: `artifact_read({ id, state: { story, interaction: "hover" } })` answers which properties change on which element; an empty delta on a column that exists is a **finding**. A changed property is not yet the proof: the proof of a hover, a press, a selection or a focus is that the fill it paints takes its container's **contour**. In a rounded control it is that control's radius less its inset; in a bar's glass, the capsule's radius less its pad on the corners facing the glass's ends and the small join on those facing a neighbour. Read it from the delta: the fill's `border-radius` and box against its container's. Prove the live piece's presses: for each press the person will make, a story of your reading mounts the same fixture, its `play` makes the press, and its frame shows the answer. Done when every frame is `drawn`, every delta says what you meant, every forced fill takes its container's contour, `moves` lists the animations each flow fires, every press on the live piece is answered (`references/proof.md`), and the live story's `parts` (`artifact_read` with `state`) name every product component the piece ships in, as `design_map` lists them under used in.
6. **Check it reads**, from each answer's `render` (labels, `unnamed`, `widthOnly`, `codeNamed`, `alike`, sections, frames per section). On the person's graph, the ask names what to do and what to pin, and every dial's label is the situation in their words. On your reading, every frame is named in plain words by its doc comment, its form (`phone 390`) from `parameters.viewport`; the frames are grouped as the person thinks of them, a `title` section per screen with one states file each, flows and `edges` as the Map face's arrows, `group` as its regions. Stories `alike` because what differs stands in a layer portalled out of the root are states of the part inside that layer: move them to that part's states file, and reach them on the live story by its press. Fix the file or the call (`artifact_update` with `ask`, `edges`, `group`) until these hold. Done when `unnamed`, `widthOnly`, `codeNamed` and `alike` are empty on both graphs.
7. **Show it** through the `show` skill. The design reaches the person as its design graph, a piece or a whole screen alike; your report says in one line what changed and what to do in it. From then on each save to the piece or its states files reaches their open graph by itself; a new round that needs their eyes again is `artifact_update` with a new `ask`. With directions, the graph holds one states file per direction, and they pick one with its Pick; read it from `artifact_read`'s `picked` (the direction's name and file). A flow they run through the running app is a preview (`does: "try"`). Read their pins with `comment_read`: each carries the frame's `state` (story, interaction, scheme, width) and the element's `file:line:column`; change that line and answer in the pin's thread.
8. **Take their tuning back into the code.** They turn your dials and retime your moves on the design graph's Controls and Animation faces. `artifact_read` answers `dials` (their value of each dial, by name) and `tuned` (each animation they retimed, `references/moves.md`). Write each into the component: a tuned transition's timing into its CSS or its motion call, a dial's value into a token or an arg default. A value that is no token yet goes into the project's token source first, then the component uses the token. A dial that names a situation (step 4) is their way through the piece and carries no value to keep. Values they set answer the judgement and reach you by themselves, no Send; after a compaction `open_items` lists each graph or preview you put to them, open or answered. Once the code carries them, close it: `question_close({ question: <its link>, as: "taken", why: <what they set> })`. Done when the code carries every accepted value, the graph, read again, draws it, and `open_items` lists it no more.

## Before you finish

**Rehearse.** Before you hand it up, read every frame yourself at each of the graph's widths, in light and dark. Read every frame as a picture (`artifact_read` with `png`, `state.width`, and `state.scheme` for the other scheme) and fix the largest fault first: framing (cut off, scaled, a plate or air nobody asked for), then hierarchy (where the eye lands first), then motion, then polish. Re-draw, and hand up once a full read finds nothing. The stage draws the picture; no browser of your own is opened for it.

**The fresh eye.** A reviewer sees only the frames and the URL, never your plan or reasons, and a finding quotes its frame.

**The report** names the person's design graph by its link and what to do in it (with directions, each by its name), what you read (widths, looks, states), what you did not check, each mock that stands for a call the server lacks, and each thing in a frame that no product component draws, with what stopped you mounting the real one.

| Check | Fails when |
| --- | --- |
| Handed live | The graph opens on anything but the live story, or a press in it does nothing |
| Mounted, not redrawn | A product component the piece ships in is missing from the live story's `parts`, or a fixture draws something a person sees or presses |
| Every state drawn | Any frame is `failed`, `empty` or `unseen` |
| Every state named | `unnamed` lists a story |
| One state, one story | Two stories differ only by their width: the hub refuses the graph |
| Each state looks its own | `alike` lists stories that draw the same picture at one width |
| Labels in their words | `codeNamed` lists a label with a word of code; or a label names a width, lists fields, or is a sentence they would not say |
| Only states that differ to the eye | A state nobody would tell from another, or one no situation of their reaches |
| A value is a dial | A position along a scale stands as a story or a direction of its own |
| The ask says what to do | It names no act, or names a file or a state code |
| Grouped as they think | One flat row where the person thinks in screens: use `title` sections, `edges`, `group` |
| A state takes its contour | A hover, focus, pressed or selected fill leaves its container's contour, or its delta is empty or names an element you did not mean |
| Moves move | A flow's `moves` entry lists no animations, or has an `error` |
| Tuning landed | `dials` or `tuned` holds a value the code does not carry |
| Directions on one graph | Two or more designs reached them as separate artifacts, or on a decision page |
| One design, one graph | A design reached them as a preview to judge, as a yes or no, or as a second artifact per width or scheme |
| One screen, one name | A screen your tree already put in the panel reached it again under a new name |
| Readings stay yours | A graph made for your own checking was opened without `reading: true` |
