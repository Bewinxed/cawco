# Design System: CawCo

**The token source is `design/tokens/cawco.resolver.json`** (W3C DTCG 2025.10, checked against the DTCG schemas on every build): its base set `cawco.tokens.json`, with the dark scheme, a coarse pointer and the compact density as modifier files. It is the one place a visual value is defined. `bun run tokens` in `apps/dashboard` builds it into `src/lib/tokens/tokens.css` (every colour that differs by scheme a `light-dark()`), the Tailwind theme `theme.css` (one utility per token, so `text-sm`, `ease-in` or `bg-red-500` names nothing) and the native apps' `Tokens.xcassets` and `Tokens.swift`. `bun run variants` writes `variants.css`. To change a value, edit the JSON and rebuild.

The frontmatter restates the shipped colours for tools that read DESIGN.md; where it and the JSON disagree, the JSON wins.

This file holds what spans screens: the principles, what the tokens mean, and the recipes of the kit's controls. It changes when the owner picks a change to the system. A decision about one screen lives in that screen's surface brief, under `apps/dashboard/.impeccable/surfaces/` (`impeccable surface-brief read <file>` finds the one for a file), and the Projects features are specified in their PRD (`.design-foundations/plans/2026-10-06-projects.md`). A rule here names its tokens and components; the code holds every measurement.

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Quiet Ledger, re-skinned for Caw&Co"**

CawCo is a board an operator glances at between other work, to answer one question: does anything need me right now? It reads like a departure board: rows hold still, numbers line up, and a quiet row means a healthy row.

The surface is warm paper by day and roost-dark at night, low in chroma. Colour is spent on five things only: the coral action, status, identity marks, the focus ring, and data past a threshold. Depth is a recessed frame holding raised content. Neither scheme is the original: every colour token picks its side through `light-dark()`, and a design is drawn and checked in both.

**Key Characteristics:**
- A coral action on warm paper; colour elsewhere only where it carries meaning.
- The frame recesses; content sits raised on it.
- Figtree for the product, JetBrains Mono for code, weights 400 and 500 only.
- A spacing ladder measured off the reference comps, not a 4px grid.
- Solar bold-duotone icons, linear only for chevrons and arrows.
- Motion that keeps one object on screen through every change and exits faster than it enters.

### Motion grammar

Motion is continuity, never decoration: "we fly instead of teleport" (Family), in Emil Kowalski's grammar as the `animate` skill states it. It stands on three curves, `linear`, and the tokens' `motion` group.

- **Ease-out** (`--ease-out`): entrances, exits, colour and any change in place; the default.
- **Ease-in-out** (`--ease-in-out`): something already on screen moving to another place, or a figure filling to a new value.
- **Drawer** (`--ease-drawer`): popovers and menus, sheets, page swaps, flights and container size changes.
- **Linear**: constant motion, a spinner's turn or a steady glide. Progress does not ease.

Two recipes the owner picked from transitions.dev run on `--ease-crossfade`, the one gentle curve, and are bound to their components alone: the skeleton's pulse and reveal, and the folder tab's choice, beats of 400ms and longer. `--ease-goo`, the one overshoot, is a comment card's pour out of its pin (PinFloat), pin and card one drawn silhouette. CSS `ease` is never used.

**The No Ease-In Rule.** No ease-in curve exists, and no run starts slow: anything that starts slow feels like the interface hesitating.

**The Frequency Rule.** How often the reader sees a motion decides whether it exists. A keyboard-initiated action never animates; the same action from a pointer keeps its motion (typing is not an action). What is seen tens of times a day (a hover, a row's highlight) gets a colour or opacity change on `--dur-control` or nothing; what is occasional (a popover, a sheet, a toast) gets the standard motion; delight belongs only to what is rare. Motion is never the only sign that something happened.

**The Fast Exit Rule.** Exits are shorter than entrances, and a thing leaves the way it came: in over `--dur-pop` or `--dur-panel`, out over `--dur-exit`; a grown surface in over `--dur-grow`, out over `--dur-grow-exit`. Nothing outlasts `--dur-panel` but a message lifted between its bubble and the field (`--dur-lift`), a grown surface and the two picked recipes; ambient loops are not beats.

**The Enter Rule.** Nothing appears from nothing. A surface arrives from `--pop-scale` or `--pop-rise` short of its place, fading in, from its trigger's side (a dialog rises `--pop-rise`); every travel is `--pop-rise`, and a view handing its place along a strip comes `--swap-nudge` from its side. A press scales to `--press-scale`. Entrances and exits are `transform` and `opacity` (a `clip-path` where a box opens out of another, `height` only for a disclosure), and no keyframe writes a length (lint `house/no-raw-motion`). The tooltip is the one exception, because it is seen tens of times a day (The Frequency Rule): it fades in over `--dur-fade` settling `--tip-rise` toward its trigger, and leaves at once.

**The One Object Rule.** What is on both sides of a change travels; it never vanishes on one side and appears on the other (`motion/share.svelte.ts`). A flight runs `--dur-panel` on the drawer curve as a transform or a clip from the source's box, the source hidden meanwhile so the screen never shows two. A surface that closes on running work travels into the place that work is parked.

**The Nothing Jumps Rule.** When a list changes, rows that stay slide, rows that leave close while fading, and rows that arrive are uncovered while what follows makes room, all from one frame (`motion/rows.svelte.ts`). A move wholly off screen jumps, so nothing drifts into view behind the reader's scroll.

**The Interruptible Rule.** Every transition turns back from where it is drawn: a state a reader can flip twice in a second is a CSS transition on `data-state`, never a keyframe restarting from zero, and what a finger lets go of settles on the one spring with the finger's velocity (`motion/spring.ts`). Predetermined motion runs off the main thread.

**The Stay In Context Rule.** The reader stays where they are and knows what is true, from asking to done. Four questions shape every flow, and the weight of the decision sets its shape:
- **Where is their attention?** The flow happens there; the thing they acted on stays in view and becomes its own result.
- **What do they need to know to decide?** The consequences this action really has, before they commit, and nothing more; a light action just happens.
- **What is true right now?** The system's state as it changes; something is done when the system says so.
- **What if it doesn't go to plan?** They are where they were, with what they had, told what happened to which thing, able to carry on.

**The Open Page Rule.** Almost nothing blurs the page or stops the reader acting elsewhere. What asks or shows opens in the page beside what it concerns, and the rest stays live. The test: if they press something else now, does anything break? A scrim or a modal hold is for the rare moment when acting elsewhere would make the open question wrong.

**The Work Outlives Its Surface Rule.** Work a surface starts that runs longer than the surface stays open (an install, a spawn, an upload, a sign-in, an update) belongs to the work, and nothing the reader does to the page loses it.
- **A reload keeps it**: the surface comes back at the same step with the same details, read from where the work lives; a secret is asked for again.
- **Dismissing parks it**: ×, Esc and a press outside never cancel the work (a modal's scrim takes no press). Closed while it runs, the surface travels into the work's one persistent place, which shows progress and then the outcome; only its own Stop or Cancel ends it.
- **One press brings it back**, exactly where it was; a finished job opens on its outcome.
- **A failure is told and recoverable**, in the error formula with a way on, until acted on; a toast never carries one.

**The Show Don't Tell Rule.** A fact with a shape is drawn, not written out: a schedule as times on a line, a budget as a meter, outcomes as marks in their status colours. Words are for what has no shape; a screen is designed as UI, never as a spreadsheet of its record.

**Live work breathes.** Anything still running pulses its opacity on `--breath` down to `--breath-floor` (the lowest at which every breather's ink holds 3:1), through the shared `breath` keyframes, on one wall clock (`@cawco/core/breath`) so every live mark breathes in step. Nothing that has finished moves.

**The One Loop Rule.** Anything that turns or blinks while pending runs `--dur-loop`, a rotation linear, a pulse ease-in-out; the skeleton's pulse is the one exception. Longer rhythms are multiples of the breath.

**The Steady Swap Rule.** A text swap never moves what stands beside it. Words change through MorphText (`components/ui/morph-text`): the box takes the larger of old and new at once and the new size once settled, so no width tweens. A label with a known second wording stands both in one cell as wide as the wider.

**The Reduced Motion Rule.** Motion is opt-in: every travelling transition sits under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`, and script asks `motionOk`. With reduced motion, fades still run and everything that travels lands; loops stop. Reduced motion means stillness, never an instant swap: what changed still fades.

**The House Helper Rule.** Each kind of motion has one helper, which already keeps the curves, clocks, interruption and reduced motion: `morph()` for a size change, `NsPopoverGroup` for sibling popovers, `reflow()` with `data-flip` for a list, `unfold` for a disclosure, `fluid-tabs` and the harness `Rail` for a switch, `keyed` for The Frequency Rule's keyboard half (`platform/motion/`, `cawco/spawn/NsPopoverGroup.svelte`, `components/ui/fluid-tabs`, under `apps/dashboard/src/lib`). A component takes a one-clock composite (`transition-control`, …) rather than listing properties and clocks; a new pairing is a new token.

### Sound and touch

**The Quiet Feel Rule.** Sound and touch accompany a gesture the reader is making; they never announce anything, never stand in for a word or a glyph, and never play for something the reader did not do. Sound is Cuelume, synthesized, always at its `subtle` emphasis, off until the reader turns it on (the Sound switch beside the theme in the rail's footer, kept per browser, `feel.svelte.ts`). Touch is web-haptics, for a finger on a coarse pointer only, with no setting. Both load on the first press or key, never with the page, and warm up while idle so the first cue costs no frame.

## Colors

Warm low-chroma paper and roost neutrals, one coral that means "you act here", and four other hues reserved for status. Every pair the token-contrast check measures (`bun scripts/token-contrast-check.ts --table`) holds its floor in both schemes.

### Primary: the coral action
- **Coral Action** (`action-solid`, `action-hover`, `on-action`): the one action colour. A primary button, Send/Stop, checked controls and the reader's own row mark fill with `action-surface`, a top-highlight gradient; its white label holds 4.5:1 on every stop.
- **Brand** (`coral-*`, `brand-solid` = `coral-9`): fills marks that carry no text (the progress bar, the caret, a vermillion section rule). White text never sits on it.
- **Brand ink and wash** (`brand-ink`, `link-ink`, `brand-wash`, `brand-ink-strong`): links, an active filter chip, peer accents.
- **Selected** (`selected-bg`, `selected-ink`, `selected-icon`): a warm apricot wash with coral ink for the selected row.
- **Chosen option** (`chip-chosen-bg`, `chip-chosen-ink`) and **focus** (`focus-ring`, `selection`).
- **Ink-solid** (`ink-solid`, `on-ink`): tooltips and inverted tiles, ink in both schemes, never coral.
- **Spark, paper and crow ink** (`spark`, `paper`, `crow-ink`): the wordmark, the mascot and brand art only, never product chrome but the app icon and the tab icon's tile.

### Tertiary: status
Four functional hues, each with a 3 / 9 / 11 step: info, warning, success and error, error on crimson so failed never reads as the coral action.
- **Live**, **Done**, **Failed** (`status-*-bg` / `status-*-ink`): a session mid-turn, finished, ended badly. Each chip holds 4.5:1.
- **Needs you** (`status-attn-glyph`, `status-attn-ink`): the one status that asks for a person. It is a mark, never a surface: the Status dot beside the word "Needs you". No card, row, chip or well is filled in its hue.
- **Idle**: no fill; rest is the absence of a chip.

**Data colour** (`data-ok`, `data-warn`, `data-bad`) keeps a hue in numerals for threshold readings (context share warns from 70%, is bad from 90%). **Syntax** (`code-*`) colours code and diffs through one theme on every code surface, each token at 4.5:1 on every code ground.

### Neutral
- **Paper ramp** (`neutral-1` … `neutral-12`): warm paper by day, roost by night, the dark ramp its own lightnesses.
- **Frame surfaces**: `surface-recess` (the field, the tray, badges, the stat well), `surface-band`, `surface-recess-deep` (a groove), `surface-shelf` (the tabs' bar), `surface-well` with `border-well`.
- **Content surfaces**: `surface-raised` (cards, menus, popovers), `surface-lift` (a thumb), `surface-hover`, `surface-fill`, `surface-pressed` (pressed or chosen, never reading as hovered), `code-bg`.
- **Edges**: `border-hairline`, `border-control`, `border-button-outline`, `border-row-outline`, `border-control-strong`, `border-contrast` (glass under Increase Contrast), `well-edge`.
- **Inks**: `ink-strong`, `ink-muted`, `ink-row` (a row's name), `ink-stat`, `ink-subtle` (the weakest that holds 4.5:1).

**The Dark Flips Polarity Rule.** At night a groove sits below the card and a thumb above it. Dark mode is a second set of lightness decisions, never an inverted light mode.

### Identity
- **Identity marks** (`mark-1` … `mark-8`, glyph `mark-glyph`): eight accent hues at one lightness under a soft sheen (`--mark-overlay`), telling sessions and subagents apart on their squares.
- **Project identity**: a project's directory hashes to one of ten hues (`identity.ts`), set through `identity-ink`, which holds 4.5:1 on a hover surface for every hue. Identity is ink, never a status dot.
- **Machine tiles** and **section hues** (`hue-*`, `machine-offline`) colour the duotone glyph on a raised icon tile.
- **Tool families** (`tool-*`): one hue per tool family at one lightness per scheme, tinting the tool glyph.
- **Accounts** (`account-*`): seven hues; accounts shown together never share one.

### Named Rules
**The Reserved Hue Rule.** Crimson, spark yellow, olive and blue mean status, and status only. Progress rings, identity and decoration take `currentColor` or an identity hue. A finished task ring is the one decoration that earns a state colour, because by then it is a result.

**The Coral Is Where You Act Rule.** Coral marks the action (one per view: the act the view is for, so a view for deciding spends it on the decision), the selected nav row, the focus ring, checked controls, the chosen option, the selection tint, and the vermillion rule that parts two regions of a glass card (Files, Caw's panel). Advice is never coral: a recommended option wears the Recommended badge, and when it starts chosen it wears both, so moving the pick moves only the coral. Coral is never a status, so it never reads as an alarm and an alarm never reads as a button.

**The White On Coral Rule.** Text on coral sits on `action-solid` or `chip-chosen-bg`, never on `brand-solid`. A new coral fill under text is measured to 4.5:1, top gradient stop included, before it ships.

**The Ink Stays Ink Rule.** Tooltips and inverted tiles are `ink-solid` with `on-ink`. They are never coral.

**The Idle Has No Fill Rule.** An idle session carries no chip. A quiet row means a healthy row, so rest is drawn as nothing.

**The No Indigo Rule.** Indigo is never a brand, action or accent hue. Identity hues span the wheel for telling things apart and are never promoted to a brand role.

**The Glyph, Word, Hue Rule.** Status is never colour alone. It reads glyph, then word, then hue (a Solar duotone glyph, then "Working", "Needs you", "Failed", "Idle", "Sleeping", "Stopped" or "Unreachable"). A mark that changes state changes shape as well as colour, and a chip carries the word inside its tint.

## Typography

**UI font:** Figtree Variable at 400 and 500 only. **Mono font:** JetBrains Mono Variable, ligatures off. **Wordmark font:** Nunito Variable (`--font-wordmark`), "Caw&Co" at weight 1000, exempt from the 500 ceiling. **Display font:** Fredoka 600, brand display only (marketing, a brand moment in an empty state), never product chrome. Every face is the repository's own file, served by `packages/core/src/house-faces.ts`, and the product's faces are inlined so the first paint never swaps fonts.

**Character:** Figtree's round forms sit near the wordmark and stay crisp at 12 to 13px in dense rows. JetBrains Mono has a tall x-height and a clear 0/O and 1/l/I in diffs; it sets large, so code runs at 12px. Ligatures are off for every code, path and ID run.

### Hierarchy
- **Meta** (400, 12px): timestamps, counts, units, hints.
- **Label** (500, 13px): names, headings, badges, chips, small buttons, menu items.
- **Body** (400, 14px): sentences and descriptions, 60 to 72ch.
- **Row** (500, 16px): a row title that is a thing to read.
- **Button** (500, 14px), **Prose** (400, 14px, 1.55, rendered markdown), **Column head** (500, 13px, uppercase).
- **Title** (500, 18→20px) and **KPI** (500, 20→24px, tabular): fluid between 390px and 1024px.
- **Code** (JetBrains Mono 400, 12px), and **Measured figure** (`mono-figure`): a measurement among Figtree words, Mono adjusted to Figtree's digit height.
- **Display** (Fredoka 600): brand only.

The dense roles keep one size at every width; they are already the smallest that reads. A role is written `type-<role>` (the whole role) or `text-size-<role>` (its size alone).

### Named Rules
**The 500 Ceiling Rule.** No product text goes above 500. Emphasis is a step from 400 to 500 or an ink step, never bold.

**The Role, Not Size Rule.** Text takes a role by what it does: names, headings, column heads and badges are label; sentences are body; timestamps, counts, units and hints are meta. Nobody picks a size and finds a role for it.

**The Tabular Figure Rule.** A figure that changes carries `.num` and is set in tabular numerals, so a ticking value never moves its neighbours; tables are tabular by default.

**The Truncate Inside Rule.** Text truncates in an inner span, never on the control or link, so the hit area is never clipped; in a row the name gives way first.

**The Row Dress Rule.** A list row has at most four dresses: its name in label and `ink-row`; the thing itself (a comment's words, a message) in body and `ink-strong`; facts about it in meta and `ink-muted`; an unread fact in `ink-strong`. A state the row's mark draws takes no word, and the name carries the row's one weight step.

**The Mark Line Rule.** Words beside a mark (an icon, a dot, an avatar, a keycap) have their x-height's middle on the mark's middle, within half a pixel, in every engine. The mark line recipe (app.css) trims each run to its x-height band and pads the room back; the kit's rows draw it, any other row takes `.mark-line`, a box on the line says `data-box`, and a mark beside wrapping words sits in a `.mark-cell`. `apps/dashboard/scripts/mark-line-check.ts` enforces it.

**The 16px Field Rule.** On a coarse pointer every text field renders at 16px or more, because iOS Safari zooms into a smaller field.

**Balanced wrapping.** Headings `text-wrap: balance`; paragraphs and list items `pretty`.

## Layout

The shell is a resizable rail (228px by default, the reader's own width wins), a 44px top bar with no ground of its own, and a page on the recessed field, padded 25px on its inline start and 21px on its end, its groups 18px apart. A page has no head under the bar: its name, where one helps, is the bar's title.

**The Title Bars Follow Liquid Glass Rule.** Every bar that heads a surface (the top bar, a pane, a side panel, a sheet, a canvas's band) follows Apple's toolbar model as Liquid Glass revised it (HIG, Toolbars, 2025-12-16), on web and native:
- **One bar, one row.** Title and controls share one line. Facts about the surface (its place, a path, an id, a count, its ask) are content, never a second line in the bar.
- **A title only where it helps.** A panel opened by its name carries none, its actions floating over its content. A title is the view's own short name, never the app's name, an id, a path or a breadcrumb.
- **A place shown as a place.** A machine is its OS mark and name; a path is never printed in a bar.
- **No bar ground.** A bar draws no fill, hairline or shadow; content runs to the top edge and the scroll edge effect blurs it under the controls, from the first scroll.
- **Floating groups of bare symbols.** At most three groups (leading, centre, trailing), each one capsule of the panel's glass. Items are bare symbols until hovered, named in their tooltip; words only where no symbol says it. Each is a BarItem, which alone draws its hover, pressed, selected and count (The Glass Contour Rule); its count is the Badge's `corner` form, taking no room. Trailing: what acts on the view, More, the prominent action, then Close (a sheet's leads, before its title).
- **One prominent action**, trailing, on `action-surface`; colour on the glass is for it and for status alone.
- **Back and Close are symbols**, named by where they go, never the words Back or Close.

**Spacing ladder.** `space-1` to `space-8`, measured off the reference comps.

**Density.** A 44px row pitch, a 32px header band and 90px stat tiles; the transcript runs a compact scale from the same tokens; under a coarse pointer compact relaxes to the touch floor.

**The Row Grid Rule.** A row's mark is centred in a 28px lead column, its words start on one text column `space-3` after it, and its last control ends on one trailing edge `space-3` in from the list's, drawn in ink: its last painted pixel (a fill, a ghost's last letter or stroke) lands on the edge within 1px, its box hanging past by what its ink stops short, never past the row's `space-3`, so a list never scrolls sideways.

**Breakpoints.** 640px (`phone:`: dialogs become bottom sheets, toasts move to the top), 900px (`desk:`: the rail becomes a burger sheet, panes take the drawer layout), and 1024px (fluid sizes reach their maximum). A coarse pointer counts as narrow for nav rows.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is tonal first and shadowed second. The frame (the app field, dialog tray, header band, segmented groove) recesses; content (cards, rows, menus, the dialog body, a thumb) sits raised on it. Shadows are warm ink tints: by day the ink at low alpha, at night the deepest surface at high alpha plus a 1px inner ring of ink (`night-ring`). Every shadow token adapts through `light-dark()`; none is keyed on the theme class.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Tile** (`shadow-tile`): cards, machine tiles, icon tiles.
- **Hairline** (`shadow-hairline`): a drop under a tile without a ring. **Stat** (`shadow-stat`): the stat tile.
- **Raised** (`shadow-raised`): a thumb in a groove, a card hanging under a row.
- **Overlay** (`shadow-overlay`, and `shadow-modal`, the same value): popovers, menus, the dialog tray, toasts, floating tags. `shadow-overlay-hung` is the overlay's drop without the night ring, for a card that hangs from its anchor.
- **Drawer** (`shadow-drawer`): sheets.
- **Drop** (`shadow-drop`, `shadow-drop-near`): a drawn shape rather than a box, as two `drop-shadow()` filters following its edge: the composer grown round a permission ask or the recall wheel.
- **Pressed inset**, **action ring** and **code ring** (`shadow-inset-sel`, `action-ring`, `code-ring`): the markdown editor's pressed button, the action and code blocks.

The Tailwind ladder has four steps, `shadow-xs`, `-md`, `-lg` and `-xl`; md, lg and xl carry the night ring.

### Stacking
What stands over what is a named role, never a number: `z-(--layer-<role>)`, the `layer` tokens, 10 apart so a role can go between two. Floating surfaces (popover, menu, submenu, select, tooltip, hover card, dialog, sheet, drawer) belong in the browser's top layer; until the kit puts them there they share `float`, the one opened last standing over the one it opened from. Order inside one component stays a plain number.

### Materials
- **Scrim**: the page's own background at 72% with a 2px blur, behind a modal dialog or sheet.
- **Panel material** (`material-panel`): the raised surface at 72% with a blur and saturation, for chrome floating over content; opaque under `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce`.
- **Composer glass**: the panel material at the base blur (`--material-blur`), so the transcript shows faintly under the field; opaque under reduced transparency.

### Named Rules
**The Frame Recesses Rule.** The frame recesses and content never does. The one sanctioned card-in-card is the stat tile's recessed well.

**The Warm Shadow Rule.** Shadows are warm ink taken from the neutral ramp, never `rgba(0,0,0,·)`. The tile's night drop is the one black, under a ring of ink.

**The Night Ring Rule.** At night a raised surface is told apart from its frame by a 1px inner ring of ink as well as its drop. The ring is part of the shadow token, so a component that takes the token gets it in both schemes.

## Shapes

Corners are graded by role, each a token: `radius-hair` for the smallest marks, `radius-xs` for marks, tiles and menu items, `radius-sm` for items inside a surface, `radius-md` for controls in the page, `radius-lg` for cards and menus, `radius-panel` for a panel, `radius-modal` for the dialog tray. Full rounding is for state pills, switches, dots and a capsule of glass.

**The Glass Contour Rule.** A control standing in a bar's glass takes the glass's contour by where it stands: its corners facing the capsule's ends take the capsule's radius less the pad, those facing a neighbour `radius-xs` (BarGlass marks each item leading, middle, trailing or only; design.css `concentric-row`). Hover, pressed, selected and focus are drawn on that one box (`concentric-at`), the ring inside it, so every state shares its contour; `state-shape` forces and reads each.

**The Concentric Rule.** A surface nested in another's corner takes the outer radius less the gap between their edges, the outer's edge counted. Where no radius token is the derived value, a derived token holds it (`inner-well-r`), never a literal. A container declares its outer radius and inset once (design.css `concentric-in`) and its rows and parts take the result (`concentric-<radius>`); the hover ghost, the selection pill and the focus ring take the contour of what they mark.

**The Measured Exception Rule.** The stat tile keeps the comp's radii (a card round its well), a panel its shell, and the smallest marks the hair. Each is a token.

**Borders are graded.** `border-hairline` separates rows and regions; `border-control` draws a control's quiet edge; `border-button-outline` and `border-row-outline` hold 3:1 for an outline button on raised and on recess. At night a border becomes a seam of ink.

**The Whole Pixel Border Rule.** A border or outline is a whole CSS pixel; a fractional stroke is an inset ring (`inset-ring-(length:--x)`) or an SVG stroke. `scripts/border-width-check.ts` refuses a fractional width.

**Folder tabs.** fluid-tabs' folder variant: the chosen tab is a sheet in the surface below it, its foot flaring into the page, the rest receding on `surface-shelf`. Each tab is one SVG path (`fold-path.ts`), so body, feet and rim move as one. The session strip's picks are in its surface brief.

**A card that hangs from its anchor** (`HangCard`, `components/ui/hang-card`). Anchor and card are one silhouette, one fill and one stroke, the anchor's flared foot running into the card's top edge on one tangent. It wraps the kit's floating surface, so focus, Escape and a press outside are the primitive's, and it slides out of the anchor's foot and back under a cut that never moves.

## Components

The kit lives in `apps/dashboard/src/lib/components/ui`. Shared recipes are the `kit-*` classes in `app.css`'s components layer; everything else a component draws is the theme's utilities and variants on its own elements.

**The Earned Count Rule.** A count on a control is drawn only above 0: at 0 the count element is gone and the control's name drops it too, on the web and in the app. A figure whose 0 is itself the reading (a measurement, a share, a total) is not a count.

### Buttons
Tactile and quiet: a hairline-edged raised block that dips under the hand.
- **Sizes:** `radius-md` at the `c-btn-h` heights; icon buttons match, in the page only (in glass, The Glass Contour Rule).
- **Outline (default):** raised, a `border-button-outline` edge, strong ink. **Primary:** `action-surface`, `on-action` text. **Secondary:** recess with a hairline. **Ghost:** transparent until hovered. **Destructive:** an `error-9` edge and `error-11` text. **Disabled:** 50%.
- **Pending:** a running button keeps its focus and place and swallows presses; its slot cross-fades to a spinner, then a check, and its label and pending wording share one cell, so it never changes width.

**The Press Rule.** A compact standalone control (a button, a chip, a switch) scales to `--press-scale`; anything wide or grouped (rows, cards, tabs, segments), and any control that opens a list, menu or card, tints to `surface-pressed` and moves nothing. A link dims. The tint outranks a control's own fill by specificity, never `!important`.

**The cursor.** An enabled button shows the pointer and a disabled or pending one the arrow, from one base style in `design.css`.

### Inputs and fields
- **Style:** `c-btn-h` tall, `radius-md`, raised surface, `border-control` edge, `shadow-xs`, body text in `ink-strong`, muted placeholder, the coral caret (`brand-solid`).
- **Focus:** the caret, and the field's own edge stepping to `border-control-strong` while its text has focus (`field-edge`, The Caret Rule). An invalid field keeps its error edge; a disabled one fades to 50%.
- **Textarea:** grows a line at a time and scrolls past its ceiling.
- **Field frame:** a frame round a borderless editor with its own controls beside it (a search with an icon, a path with Browse, a prompt with its chips) is the field: its edge steps while the text inside has focus, not while a chip or button does.
- **Select trigger:** the input's look with a sort-vertical glyph.

### Cards and the stat tile
- **Card:** `radius-lg`, raised surface, the tile shadow, no border.
- **Stat tile** (`StatTile.svelte`): a 10px card holding a recessed well 7px in (The Measured Exception Rule): the label over the value in the KPI role, `ink-stat`, its unit in meta on the same baseline. A new value morphs digit by digit (The Steady Swap Rule).

### Badges, chips and pills
- **Badge:** `radius-xs`, label role, recess; `live`, `done` and `fail` take their status pairs, `attn` its Status dot before the word.
- **State pill:** fully round, status tint, no glyph; Needs you wears the Status dot instead of a tint, idle wears nothing.
- **Setting chip:** `radius-sm`, a glyph and a chevron. A setting that can't change where it is shown is the same chip sunk (`surface-recess`, `border-hairline`, no chevron), never plain text.
- **Option chip:** the one way an answer is picked: a keycap digit; picked, the coral edge, a recessed fill and a vermillion keycap (`chip-chosen-bg`), never the attn hue.
- **Inline chip** (ui/chip `inline`): a reference set in running text, its mark then its name and state in words, stopping at `c-inline-chip-max-w`; it keeps its words and box from its first paint. Each kind's look is one entry in `cawco/reference-looks.ts`.

### Navigation, menus and floating surfaces
- **Popover, dropdown, select, context menu, toast** (`kit-pop`): `radius-lg`, `border-control` edge, overlay shadow, raised. It opens from its trigger's side over `--dur-pop` on the drawer curve and leaves over `--dur-exit`; a change of size takes `morph()`.
- **Submenu:** the same surface, portalled, beside its row, or hanging from its row's foot where neither side holds it (`submenu-side.ts`).
- **Menu item:** `radius-xs`, the touch floor on a coarse pointer, label role. A chosen item shows its tick in its own ink; `surface-hover` is the only fill it takes.
- **Hover ghost and selection pill** (`highlight.svelte.ts`): one of each per list, drawn under its rows. The ghost is `surface-hover` and glides to the row under the pointer; the pill glides to a new selection, and a row picked under the ghost becomes the pill at once. No ghost on touch.
- **Limit bar** (`usage/LimitBar`): a track, a fill in its state colour (`meter-*`), a pace tick; it says whether it reads used or left.
- **Ticks and dots:** a checkbox tick draws itself along its stroke; a radio dot grows from 0.6 as it fades in; with reduced motion both only fade.
- **Table** (`ui/table`): a ledger under a `surface-band` header band in the column-head role, figures tabular. Where the table's own width is a phone's (`@container/table`), each row becomes a two-line card.

### Segmented controls and tabs
- **Segmented** (`tabs/thumb.ts`, one recipe for Tabs, single toggle groups and fluid-tabs' track): a `surface-recess-deep` groove holding a lifted thumb, thumb and segments concentric with it (`--shape`), `radius-md` round `c-segmented-pad`. In glass it is BarSegments: the glass its list, each segment a BarItem. Segments are label weight, muted until chosen. The thumb glides, position and size together, from where it is drawn. A segment presses with the tint, never a scale.
- **Fluid tabs** (`fluid-tabs`): the segmented groove, its active segment a lifted sheet that slides; a scrolling track fades the edge with more to scroll and glides sideways under a wheel on the `--dur-glide` spring. Arrow keys move and choose at once.
- **Folder tabs:** fluid-tabs' folder variant on `surface-shelf` (Shapes, Folder tabs).

### Tooltip
Solid ink, never coral: `ink-solid` fill and arrow, `on-ink` text, meta role, `radius-sm`, with a rotated square arrow. It fades in over `--dur-fade` settling `--tip-rise` toward its trigger and leaves at once (The Enter Rule names it the one exception).

### Asking in the page
The one way to ask before doing (`AskHere.svelte`, `ask-here.svelte.ts`). The question unfolds in the page's flow under the control that asked it, indented to its row's title: a raised tile holding the title in label, the consequence in meta, then Cancel (ghost) and the verb (destructive, or the grant's shield). It takes the focus on Cancel. The verb stays pending until its work ends; a refusal stays under the question in the refuser's words, and only work that went closes it. An action that reaches several things names them inside the question, read live.

### Dialog, sheet and drawer
For the rare holds The Open Page Rule allows; no confirmation is a dialog.
- **Dialog:** a recess tray (`radius-modal`) holding a raised body over the scrim, Close a ghost icon in its corner. It enters over `--dur-panel` on ease-out, rising `--pop-rise`, and leaves over `--dur-exit`.
- **Sheet:** a raised edge panel with the drawer shadow and Close leading its header, sliding its whole size on the drawer curve. A finger drags it 1:1, released past 30% or on a flick onto the spring; a mouse uses Close or Escape.
- **Drawer (vaul):** every bottom sheet: full-bleed, `radius-lg` top corners, a grabber, one header (Close, then the title; the touch floor under a finger), content-tall up to 85vh.

**The Hold-To-Select Rule.** A press that starts in a text field is never captured by a drawer or gesture round it, so iOS hold-to-select and Paste reach the field. A held press on a context-menu trigger suppresses the platform callout.

### Toast
The floating surface over sonner, body type. It enters from `--pop-rise` on its edge's side and leaves toward it; under a finger it tracks 1:1 and flicks away. On a desk toasts sit in the content area's bottom-right, never over the rail; on a phone at the top under the bar, clear of the composer. A toast never paints over navigation and never carries a failure.

### Alert
A compact tinted row, never a slab: `radius-md`, body type, an optional glyph spanning title and description. Variants: default (recess), destructive, warning, success and info (each its status pair). No border.

### Empty, loading and skeleton states
- **Empty** (`ui/empty`): a duotone mark, a title, one body line saying why, and its one action in words (The Empty Starts Work Rule).
- **Skeleton** (`ui/skeleton`): a `surface-fill` block at exactly the size of what it stands for, pulsing on one clock, and giving way in the reveal (motion/curves `reveal`).
- **Spinner:** a ring with a quarter arc on the loop, for a pending button, a toast, or a wait under the skeleton's threshold.

**The Empty Starts Work Rule.** An empty state always offers to start the work that would fill it: its one action starts a session with Caw (or the screen's own agent) already asking for the thing, its words editable before they go. Where that can't start, it says why and offers the way by hand.

**The Caw Stands On The Axis Rule.** Caw sits in the 28px lead column only on a row. Everywhere else (an empty page, a hero, a setup card, a fault at a surface's centre) he stands centred on the composition's own axis.

**The Claim Of Nothing Rule.** An empty state renders only once the data is known to be empty. While it loads, the skeleton stands at the real layout's size, and the first read replaces it in the reveal. Nothing claims zero while the hub connection is down.

**The Real Wait Rule.** Nothing stands in for a wait until it has lasted `--dur-wait-grace`. Caw, once he appears, finishes his enter before what arrives lands, then fades out from the drawing he is on (assets/mascot/README.md, Contract).

### Status displays
- **Session status:** a Solar duotone glyph alone, no circle round it, one per state (`workspace/session-status.ts`); then the word in label, tinted live, attn or fail where it applies. The Working glyph breathes.
- **Live dot** (`ui/live-dot`): work in progress is never a line of words alone. Words that say what is running stand beside the live dot, a `status-dot-size` circle in `status-live-glyph` that breathes, on the mark line; where something already marks the wait (a spinner, Caw, a step's glyph) it needs none. Where a speaker is at work, the dot stands on its mark's top-end corner, cut round by `status-dot-cut`, and the words go. One breath everywhere: every live dot is on the shared breath.
- **Task ring:** wound from the top as a clock is read, in `currentColor`. With no plan it turns an arc on the loop; with a plan the arc eases to the share done; finished, it becomes a check. Nothing planned and nothing running draws nothing.

### Identity marks and tiles
- **Identity mark:** a square at `radius-xs` in a `mark-N` colour under the sheen, the vendor glyph in `mark-glyph`.
- **Tree mark** (`TreeMark.svelte`): the tile leading every row a list nests rows under. A parent with a count shows it on the tile, on a deck of copies behind it; the mark is the switch that opens its rows, its face giving way to the chevron under the pointer and while open.
- **Session row** (`SessionRow.svelte`): one row for a session in every list: mark, title with its status read out first, age at the trailing edge; the session menu's trigger, draggable to split. A list owns its container, order and nesting.
- **Working indicator** (`motion/echo`): a working tile echoes outside itself for three loops, a list's rows beating top to bottom on one clock, then rests.
- **Status dot:** the one status shape on a tile and Needs you's mark wherever it stands: a `status-dot-size` circle on the tile's top-right corner, the tile cut round it (`status-dot-cut`). Needs you echoes three loops, then stills. No ring stands round a tile.
- **Icon tile:** a raised square at `radius-sm` with the tile shadow and a duotone glyph; chosen, it inverts to `ink-solid`.

### Icons
**The Solar Duotone Rule.** Every product icon is Solar's bold-duotone cut, imported through `$lib/icons.ts` (packages/icons); a bar's symbols, chevrons, arrows and CopyButton take Solar's linear cut (`…Linear`, house/bar-symbol-linear). The duotone's second layer is `currentColor` at 50%. A status glyph keeps its `status-*-glyph` hue and holds 3:1 as a whole on its surfaces. Close, Plus and Tick are local glyphs on Solar's 24px grid, a 1.5 stroke with round caps; the Tick is also the done glyph, in `status-done-glyph`. An icon is the glyph alone, no container round it; where Solar ships only a contained cut, a bare one is drawn in its style.
- **Sizes:** 12px beside meta text, 16px in controls and rows (the default), 20px in nav and headers.

### Focus and hit areas
**The Caret Rule.** A text field's focus indicator is its caret (WCAG 2.2 Understanding 2.4.7): no field, editor or search strip draws the coral ring. A field with a frame steps that frame's edge to `border-control-strong` while its text has focus (`field-edge`); a field that is the whole surface shows its caret alone.

**The One Ring Rule.** Every control shows one ring on keyboard focus only (`:focus-visible`): a 2px outline in `focus-ring`, 2px outside, never a box-shadow, so it follows the radius and survives forced colours; inset on rows, tabs and anything flush or clipped. The ring is for focus alone: a selection or a mode takes the selected marks instead.

**The 44 Touch Rule.** On a coarse pointer every control's tappable area reaches 44×44 through a transparent `::after` (`.touch-hit`) while its drawn size stays; neighbours stop at the midpoint (`--hit-gap-x`, `--hit-gap-y`). A text field's area lies under it (`.hit-under`). A chip in running text takes its line's height, as a link does (WCAG 2.5.8's inline exception).

**The 24 Pointer Rule.** On a fine pointer a control drawn under 24×24 reaches 24px through `.pointer-hit`.

**Hover only where it exists.** Hover sits under `(hover: hover)`, so a tap never leaves a stuck hover.

### Answering a permission
**The Peer Rule.** Approve and Deny are peers on every surface that answers a permission (Caw's panel, the transcript's card `transcript/Prompt.svelte`, the peek): the operator answers under time pressure, often from a phone, and a rushed thumb must not grant when it meant deny. They are the same size (`sm`, with a 44×44 hit area on every pointer), the same label type, the same `surface-recess` fill and `border-button-outline` edge, in `ink-strong`, reading apart by glyph, word and place, never by hue; no status fill or ink touches either, and neither changes under the pointer. They stand at opposite ends of their row, at least `--space-8` apart, with no third control between them.

**The Consequential Grant Rule.** A standing grant ("Always allow … for …") is wider than the request, so it is kept apart: under a break and a hairline, after a sentence saying what it would allow, in the warning tint with warning ink, a shield glyph and a real edge, the most salient control on the card on purpose. Above the answer row the card says who asks, what will happen and to what, and the change itself, open (the diff, scrolling inside). Nothing is behind a disclosure: a grant is made on what it changes.

## Voice

The words the product shows live in `packages/core/src/words/**`, their one source, and are written to these rules.

**The Should It Exist Rule.** Before a string is phrased, it earns its place on the screen (the `clean-copy` skill). Walk it down in order and cut at the first that fires: a thing the person would never have wondered about, unprompted, goes, since naming it plants the question; a caption that restates its label, field or value goes; a line that rules out a failure, defends a choice or remembers a fixed bug goes (the fix is the fix). Cut whole clauses; when nothing is left, the element goes with its words, its empty hint prop and its `<p>` too.

What survives is what the person cannot act without:
- a real consequence at the moment of choosing ("Anything not yet synced is lost."), without the permission slip about when to press;
- a scope or compatibility fact they can't see;
- what a blank state means and what to do next;
- the unit, scale or format of a bare value.

A string needed only in a rare state stands in that state, not on the page at rest.

- **Precise.** Name the exact thing, never a category: "nixbox stopped answering (timeout)", not "Something went wrong with a machine." A number beats a word ("3 sessions", not "a few"), and the target comes before the effect.
- **Calm.** Say what is true and the next step. No alarm inflation, no empty reassurance, no humour in an error, warning, confirm or permission ask.
- **Commanding.** Active voice, the imperative for actions. No "we" as the product, no "I" for it: name the harness or "the agent".
- **Honest.** Say when the product doesn't know ("no machine has reported" is not "reported zero") and when an action cannot be undone. A grant is never made more attractive: "Approve", never "Allow all".

**Punctuation.** No `!` on an error, warning or wait, and `Success!` never ships. A full stop ends a sentence that stands alone; a label, button, chip, status word or figure takes none. An ellipsis marks only work in progress ("Syncing…"), a row that asks first ("Forget project…") or a placeholder.

**Contractions.** Preferred in body copy, empty states and confirmations ("doesn't", "can't"). Never where the negation is the point: "cannot be undone" stays two words.

**Error formula.** What happened, why (when it isn't obvious), how to fix it, what happens next; the first and third are always there. No "we" as the subject, no blame ("you entered"), no bare code: a code goes in parentheses after the words ("(502)"). Say what is safe ("The run is paused and holds its place."). It sits beside the problem, and a failure of work that outlives its surface stays until acted on. "nixbox stopped answering (timeout). The run is paused and holds its place. Check the machine, then Reconnect."

**Empty-state formulas.**
- **First use:** what goes here and why, and the one action that starts it. Never "No sessions yet."
- **No results:** "No [noun] for '[query]'", the query verbatim, and another way on.
- **Cleared by the person:** acknowledge it and name the next action, never the first-use words.
- **Error:** what should be here, why it isn't, and Retry.

**Button labels.** Verb + object, clear with the text around it hidden: "Delete session", "Start session", "Ask Caw" ("Ask" takes its object by name). Never "Submit", "OK", "Yes" or "Click here"; "Continue" only where the next step is in view. A destructive confirm names the consequence and its permanence, and its button names the act: "Delete this session? Its transcript and permissions are removed. This cannot be undone." → Delete session.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** take every visual value from the tokens, adding a token before any one-off value.
- **Do** check every change in both schemes, at 1440 and 390.
- **Do** keep one coral act per view, and say status by glyph and word before hue.
- **Do** carry shared objects across a change and take the house helper for each motion.
- **Do** shape each flow by The Stay In Context Rule and open it in the page.
- **Do** put a decision about one screen in that screen's surface brief, and a change to the system here.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use coral for a status, a status hue for an action or decoration, or indigo anywhere.
- **Don't** set text above 500, on `brand-solid`, or under 16px in a field on a coarse pointer.
- **Don't** use an ease-in curve, animate a keyboard action, or let an exit outlast its entrance.
- **Don't** tell a failure in a toast, or lose work a surface started.
- **Don't** ship copy that fails The Should It Exist Rule.
- **Don't** use a `group-has-*` utility in the dashboard: one such rule makes every element put into the page restyle the whole rail. Style from a state the component sets on itself or its group (`group-data-*`), or a sibling.
